# The Job Right Now

I've sat down to write about AI and it's impact on Software Engineering many times, but every time it feels like I have little to add. Partly because I feel many others are doing a better job expressing the sort of  [frustrations I have](https://minutes.substack.com/p/tool-shaped-objects), and giving the sort of [advice I find helpful](https://addyosmani.com/blog/cognitive-surrender/). The other reason is because I don't even feel I know what _I_ think about AI and it's impact on my field. It feels much easier to "offload my thinking" on AI, not to AI this time, but to others who are much smarter, more articulate than I am.

On the flip side, I've said before that [writing helps me think](./excuses), and I believe right now learning and practicing thinking for yourself is on of the most, if not _the most_ valuable ability to cultivate right now.

## Chill out, let people get there theirselves

There's a trick to this though: thinking for yourself doesn't mean "be a contrarian", it doesn't mean you have to have a new, and original take. The discourse kind of sucks right now, because _everyone_ is figuring this out at all at the same time. Some people have been using AI for programming longer, and so some of those people have been thinking about this longer. There's a temptation to downplay other people's "ah ha" moments because it's a "tired" take in your little sphere of the internet. Anyway, that's a more personal, insecurity-laden reason I haven't spent time writing about my career yet.

I remember when I first started gaining some competence in my field. I was particularly focused on React, and hooks had just come out. I felt very superior because I knew all the hooks by heart, and I could implement and explain the proper (and more importantly, improper) way to implement a `useEffect`. I had this knowledge that at the time made me a pretty decent web developer within a React codebase. I even gave some little local meetup talks that I thought were pretty good.

But then I would see other people talk about hooks! And even if they were right, I would just sit there thinking:

> Breaux, what is this hooks for BABIES?! You suck, you're just repackaging the docs and Ryan Florence's 2018 hooks talk. Why would you even waste time adding this blog post/video/talk. 

Of course, I was doing the exact same thing.

This is a really immature way of thinking about learning. In the digital age, putting some words or even a video on the internet is _basically_ free. Even if some content is just a recapitulation of something else, it's:
1. not a zero-sum game, the surface area of learning can basically only increase
2. maybe the first time someone else has heard this idea, concept, learning
3. probably most beneficial for the learning of the person who shared

There are 2 important caveats:
1. Don't plagiarize, state your sources, especially if you're a Dan Abramov cover band
2. Try very hard not to be blatantly incorrect.

That second used to be a lot easier to do successfully, at least it seemed that way. A lot of blogs, videos, and talks were about "how to do X", "how to use this API", "what is Functional Programming". For the most part, there were some definitely right and wrongs, and when something was someone's opinion or personal preference, it was easy to show that and then let people think and try it for themselves

But with AI for programming, it's different: there is no right or wrong. To speak, to predict, to advice _is_ to be wrong right now. If you're not wrong now, you'll be wrong in 6 months. The landscape of frontier models, agentic harnesses, best practices, and YNGMI X-articles so rapidly changing that many feel they have a second job right now, which is to just keep up.

## I'm not having fun

I oscillate through basically 3 modes:
1. I'm overwhelmed, I'm not having fun
2. Holy heck, this is awesome, I've never been so productive or built something so cool
3. This is alright
