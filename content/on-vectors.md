# On Vectors (my mental model for using AI)

Physics was never my strong suite. I studied mathematics at university, but vectors, matrices, linear algebra were always my weakest points (bum dum, _cymbal sound_).

One thing I do remember is that vectors have both a magnitude and direction.

This is how I think about using AI.

If we're done bullshitting ourselves about the [capabilities of LLMs to replace human value](https://www.fastcompany.com/91551736/openai-ceo-sam-altman-makes-a-lot-of-predictions-heres-how-theyve-fared-so-far), I'd like to propose a working mental model to keep in your human mind when using these systems. You can put it in your [`AGENTS.md`](./stop-using-claude) if you want, but I don't think it will be very helpful.

AI (LLMs, agents, image generation, whatever) are _all_ magnitude. Magnitude like we've never seen. Magnitude to the max. Build me a million dollar sass no mistakes in a day kind of magnitude. It's why so many people were bamboozled into thinking [something big was happening](https://shumer.dev/something-big-is-happening). _Something_ is certainly happening, we're still figuring that out and most predictions are happening. But the something that is always happening is pretty much always **big**. AI is great at big, if you allow me stretch bigness to mean magnitudinal.

A chainsaw, a small combination of engine and metal, can fell the largest tree. It can also cut a pencil at the risk of cutting off your foot.

Okay, enough metaphors.

If you've used AI _even a little bit_, you've seen the magnitude. Sometimes the point A to B that a token-creation algorithm takes you is amazing, even better than what you could have done yourself at least not without significantly more time. It sometimes it's a waste $2.05 followed by a shameful `git reset --hard`.

A vector, no matter how big the magnitude, will never get to the desired B without the proper direction.

You are the direction.

Everyone is talking about how we're the orchestrator of our own little AI teams. Maybe that's a helpful mental model for you, it's not for me. We're already seeing [the limits and burnout of orchestration mindset](https://addyosmani.com/blog/orchestration-tax/), and I say this as someone who very often is running multiple agents in parallel. We're all harness creators, agent-puppet-masters, tokenmaxxers. We're all magnitude, magnanimously magnifying the majestic multitude of (large language) models. LLMs can go far, we can make them go farther, and in parallel to work going just as far. Where is it going? Doesn't matter.

I think the industry is moving on from this thinking. It's time to get back to direction. 

> Give me a lever long enough and a fulcrum on which to place it, and I shall move the world.
> -Archimedes

It's time to get back to thinking about where we're going. I'm hardly the first to point this out. Building things was always a hard part, but never the most important part (we engineers want to think it is, because it's the part we do). What to build is probably the most important part. It's the drummer, keeping the beat to which all the musicians of engineering, design, marketing, etc. keep time to.

As an engineer, you're still responsible for _what_ is built. You're responsible for getting to B. You can keep throwing magnitude all you want, but without direction you won't get to B.
